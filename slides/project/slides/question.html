1 · The question

Who pays for Europe’s natural catastrophes?

Three questions, each with a measurable answer

  1. How large is the gap between economic and insured losses, by peril, and how has it moved since 1980?
  2. Which countries and country–peril combinations are least covered?
  3. Who bears the uninsured share, and does the gap follow exposure or the design of the insurance system?

Protection gap

1 − insured losses ÷ economic losses

Why it matters. Damage depends on hazard × exposure × vulnerability. Insurance leaves the damage unchanged and decides who pays: households, firms or governments.

Course link: IPCC (2014); Seghini (2026), §3.2

Three questions. One: how big is the gap and has it changed. Two: where is it worst. Three: who bears it and what goes with a smaller gap. The definition is EIOPA’s own: one minus insured over economic losses. Insurance does not reduce the damage, it decides who pays it.
