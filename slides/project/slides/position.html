11 · Our position

Compulsory cover, backed by public reinsurance

Demand is too low: households are myopic about rare events, and ad-hoc relief invites charity hazard. Supply is costly where losses hit everyone at once. Three of the course’s instrument families respond:

Mandate

Works only if affordable and enforced. Romania’s PAID: low uptake, cover capped at €10–20k per home.

Public support

State or EU reinsurance of the catastrophic layer makes supply affordable.

Price signal

Flat premiums do not lower the gap on their own and create moral hazard. Keep some risk pricing, tied to prevention.

This is the logic of the ECB–EIOPA proposal. It would matter most for flood and earthquake in the South and East.

Our position. Low demand comes from myopia and underestimating rare risks, plus charity hazard where relief is ad hoc. Costly supply comes from correlated losses. A mandate answers demand, but Romania shows it needs affordability and enforcement: PAID policies are capped at 10 to 20 thousand euros, uptake is low and 25 to 75% of people find cover unaffordable. Public reinsurance answers supply. On flat premiums, consistent with the econometrics slide: they go with low gaps only because they come bundled with compulsion and public schemes; alone they do not lower the gap, and they remove the price signal for prevention. So: compulsory cover, public reinsurance of the top layer, premiums that keep some risk pricing.
