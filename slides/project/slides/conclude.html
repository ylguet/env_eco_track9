Why it is not causal

Countries chose their schemes (selection); disasters create schemes (reverse causality); the regime is a 2025 snapshot.

What would make it causal

A difference-in-differences around a reform: Italy’s mandatory cover for firms (2025), Romania’s PAID (2009–10), Belgium’s flood-in-fire-policy reform.

Three descriptions and one correlation. The trend is now an actual estimate on annual data, not just “we cannot tell”: the share is stable and the trend is not significant. The regression is a correlation: schemes were not assigned at random, big disasters create schemes, and the regime is a 2025 snapshot applied to losses since 1980. A causal design: Italy since 2025 obliges non-agricultural firms to insure against earthquake, flood and landslide, with the public reinsurer SACE taking up to half of the claims. Comparing Italian firms’ gap before and after, against similar countries, is a clean future test. Historical candidates: Romania’s PAID and Belgium’s inclusion of flood in fire policies in the mid-2000s.
